Fence Company in Larimer County, CO

Unincorporated Larimer County is not the City of Fort Collins with a different headline. City height limits, alley setbacks, and the residential ban on barbed wire do not automatically follow a parcel outside the city. County lots are larger, gates are wider, and ditch crossings show up on the line.

What fencing looks like here

Call with the address, not only the nearest town. Once we know the parcel is outside a city, we stop using that city’s fence sheet and price the line that is actually there.

  • Confirm the jurisdiction first. A Fort Collins mailing address can still be county land, and the fence rules change at that line.
  • Agricultural fencing, brace assemblies, and drive gates are a larger share of county work than patio privacy.
  • Ditches, laterals, and access easements have to stay workable. A fence that blocks a ditch rider or a shared drive is a bad layout.
  • Wind and longer spans mean post spacing and gate posts are designed for the run, not copied from a 50-foot backyard.
  • House yards on county parcels can still take wood, vinyl, or chain link. That screen is a separate run from the field fence.

Questions about fencing in Larimer County

Do Fort Collins fence height rules apply in unincorporated Larimer County?

No. The city’s 4-foot front-yard and 6-foot rear-yard limits are city rules. County parcels follow county and zoning requirements, which we check for that address. A Fort Collins mailing address is not proof the house is in the city.

Can you cross or work around an irrigation ditch?

We can fence up to a ditch and leave the access the ditch needs. We do not block a lateral to steal a few feet of yard. Tell us where the water runs. It changes post placement more than people expect.

What gate do county driveways usually need?

A leaf that clears the trucks and trailers that actually use the drive, hung on a post that stays plumb in the wind. Width is measured from the equipment. A standard 10-foot yard gate is often short on a county lane.
