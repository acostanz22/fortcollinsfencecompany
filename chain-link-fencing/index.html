Chain Link Fence Installation in Fort Collins, CO

Chain link is the practical enclosure: galvanized or coated fabric, sized for a backyard, a dog run, or a commercial lot. It does not block a view. It does cover a lot of footage without the cost of a solid wall, and the gauge has to match the use.

When chain link is the right fence

Use chain link when you need containment or a secure edge and you do not need privacy.

Dog yards

A 4- or 5-foot residential fabric with a buried or tight bottom holds most dogs better than a picket with gaps.

Long residential lines

Side and rear lines where looks matter less than a straight, tight fence.

Commercial perimeters

Heavier wire, taller fabric, and heavier terminal posts. A backyard roll is the wrong product on a yard that sees trucks.

Not a privacy fence

Slats add screening and also add wind load. If the goal is a full visual block, wood or vinyl is the direct answer.

How the work goes

  1. 1. Pick the spec Height, gauge, coating, and whether this is a house or a commercial lot. Those are the quote.
  2. 2. Set terminal and line posts Ends, corners, and gates get the heavier posts. Line posts are spaced for the fabric, not stretched to save one post.
  3. 3. Stretch the fabric Fabric is tensioned, tied, and finished with a top rail or tension wire so it does not belly in the first wind.
  4. 4. Hang the gate Gate frames are welded assemblies, not a piece of fabric on hinges. The latch is checked against the grade.

What changes the price

We do not publish a per-foot rate. These are the items that move an estimate for this fence.

Height and gauge

Taller, heavier fabric costs more and needs stronger posts. Residential 11-gauge and a commercial spec are not the same fence.

Coating

Galvanized is the base. Vinyl-coated black or green costs more and is what many HOAs will accept.

Rails and privacy slats

A top rail is standard on most house jobs. Slats add cost and wind load.

Gates

Walk and drive gates are framed steel. They are a separate line on the quote.

Layout

Corners, grade changes, and a rocky post hole add time on an otherwise simple fabric job.

Questions about chain link fencing

How much does chain link cost for a yard?

It is usually the lower-cost way to enclose a large area, and the number still depends on height, gauge, coating, and gates. A 4-foot galvanized backyard and a 6-foot black-coated line with two gates should not be quoted as one rate. We measure and price the spec you actually need.

Can you add privacy slats?

Yes. Slats cut the view and they turn the fence into more of a sail, so we do not add them to a light, loose line. If privacy is the real goal, a wood or vinyl screen is often the cleaner quote. Slats make sense when you want chain link first and a partial screen second.

Do you offer residential and commercial chain link?

Yes. A house yard gets a residential gauge, a top rail, and a walk gate. A lot, yard, or site gets heavier fabric, heavier terminals, and gates built for vehicles if trucks use the opening. We will not put a backyard roll around a commercial yard.

Do I need chain link if I want to block a view?

No. Chain link is see-through on purpose. It contains and marks. If the neighbor’s window is the problem, look at a privacy fence. If the dog and the property line are the problem, chain link is the direct tool.

Can you tighten a loose line or replace one bent post?

Yes. Loose fabric can often be re-tensioned if the posts are still plumb. A bent line post is a replacement. A bent terminal or a gate post that leans usually means that corner gets rebuilt. We repair the failed part when the rest of the mesh is still sound.
