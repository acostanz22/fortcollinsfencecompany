Fence Repair in Fort Collins, CO

Most repair calls are a leaning post, a few broken boards, loose chain link, or a gate that will not latch. If the rest of the line is straight and the posts are holding, we repair that section. If frost, rot, or rust has taken the posts, we will say the line needs to be rebuilt instead of selling a patch.

When a repair is the right job

Repair the fence when the failure is local and the rest of the line still has life.

After wind or a wet spring

A panel blown off sound posts, or a gate that heaved and now drags.

Rot at the ground

Wood posts that failed at grade while the boards above are still straight.

Impact

A backing vehicle, a fallen limb, or a dog that finally won against one bay.

Not a full reset

If every post is out of plumb, patching one bay leaves you with the same fence next winter.

How the work goes

  1. 1. Look at the failure Posts, rails, boards or fabric, and the gate. A photo helps. The quote still depends on what we find in the ground.
  2. 2. Say repair or replace We tell you which posts can stay. That is the decision that changes the price.
  3. 3. Fix the failed part New post, new bay, re-stretched fabric, or a rebuilt gate leaf. The sound fence stays.
  4. 4. Check the latch and the line The repair is done when the gate closes and the fixed bay matches the height of the fence next to it.

What changes the price

How many posts have failed

One post is a repair. A run of rotten posts is closer to a new fence.

Material

Matching cedar, a vinyl panel profile, or a chain-link gauge can take time if the original is older.

Concrete in the hole

Pulling an old footing is part of a post replacement.

Gates

Hardware-only repairs are smaller. A twisted frame or a broken hinge post is larger.

Access

A backyard with no gate wide enough for materials takes longer than a line along an alley.

Questions about fence repair

How much does a fence repair cost versus a new line?

A repair is priced by the failed posts, bays, and gates, not by the length of the whole fence. If only one corner heaved, replacing the fence is the more expensive choice and usually the wrong one. If the posts are soft at the ground all the way down the line, a repair quote and a replacement quote can land in the same range. We will show you both when that is true.

Can you tell if the posts are still worth keeping?

Yes. We look at plumb, at the ground line, and at whether the post moves when the fence is pushed. A stained board can look terrible while the posts are fine. A pretty board on a rotten post is the opposite. The post decides it.

Do you repair wood, vinyl, chain link, and metal?

Yes. Wood boards and posts, cracked vinyl panels, loose or climbing chain link, and bent or rusted metal bays. We also repair the gates on all of those. If a material is discontinued, we will say whether a close match exists before we start.

Do I need a full replacement after one hard winter?

Not by default. Freeze-thaw in Fort Collins pushes posts, and a single winter can rack a gate without killing the fence. Reset what moved. Replace what broke. A full new line is for fences that were already at the end of the posts, not for one wind event.

Can you fix a gate that no longer latches?

Yes. That is one of the most common calls. Sometimes the latch moved with the post, sometimes the hinges pulled out, and sometimes the leaf swelled or racked. We adjust or rebuild the opening. If the fence on both sides is leaning, the latch repair will not hold until those posts are addressed.
