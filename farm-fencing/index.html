Farm and Horse Fence Installation near Fort Collins, CO

Farm fencing is a different build from a backyard panel. Runs are longer, gates have to clear a truck or a trailer, and the fence has to match the animal. A horse fence and a cattle fence do not use the same wire, and a city lot in Fort Collins is not the place for barbed wire.

When you need farm fencing

This is the fence for acreage, pastures, and the edge between a house and open ground.

Horses

Horses need a visible fence and a mesh or rail they are less likely to catch a hoof in. Barbed wire is a poor horse fence.

Cattle and pasture

High-tensile wire, with proper braces at ends and corners, holds a longer line than a wood privacy panel ever will.

Equipment gates

A 12-foot opening that looks fine on paper will not clear a truck with mirrors. We measure the machine, not a guess.

Wind on open ground

North toward Wellington and east toward Windsor, a solid panel acts like a sail. Wire and rail let the wind through.

How the work goes

  1. 1. Name the animal and the gate Horses, cattle, or a property-line marker. Then the widest machine that has to pass.
  2. 2. Brace the ends Corners, ends, and gate posts are built as braces. A long wire run fails at the ends first.
  3. 3. String and tension Wire is tensioned to the spec for that material. Too tight on a cold day and too loose in July are both mistakes.
  4. 4. Hang the gate level Farm gates sag when the post is light. The hinge post is set for the leaf and the wind, then the latch is checked from the tractor side.

What changes the price

We do not publish a per-foot rate. These are the items that move an estimate for this fence.

Length

Farm jobs are priced by the run. A 400-foot pasture edge is not a backyard.

Animal and material

Pipe-and-rail, woven wire, and high-tensile are different materials and different post spacing.

Corners and braces

Every change of direction needs a brace, and braces are a larger share of a short, crooked line.

Gates

Width, single or double, and whether the gate has to be chain-latched for livestock.

Ground

Rock, ditch crossings, and soft ground along the Poudre change how posts go in.

Questions about farm fencing

How much does a horse or cattle fence cost?

Price follows length, corners, and gates more than it follows a catalog photo. A straight high-tensile run is the lower-cost pasture fence. Pipe, wood rail, and a 14-foot gate move it up. We walk or measure the line before we quote, because a map undercounts corners and ditches.

Can you build a fence a horse is less likely to get a hoof through?

Yes. Woven wire with a visible top rail, or a rail fence with tight enough spacing, is the usual answer. A single smooth wire is not a horse fence. We will not use barbed wire for horses. If the pasture also holds cattle, we will say which fence can do both and which cannot.

Do you offer pipe, rail, wire, and high-tensile?

Yes. Pipe and wood rail for horses and for the look of a property entrance. Woven wire where animals need a mesh. High-tensile where the job is a long, straight cattle or boundary line. The material is picked from the animal and the span, not from one default.

Do I need barbed wire inside Fort Collins?

On a typical residential lot, no. The city prohibits barbed wire except in employment and industrial zoning. A pasture in unincorporated Larimer County is a different rule set than a lot inside Fort Collins or Loveland. We confirm where the parcel sits before anyone talks about barbed wire.

Can you hang a gate wide enough for a truck or trailer?

Yes. That is most of the farm-gate work: an opening that clears the trailer, a hinge post that stays plumb, and a latch you can reach without climbing. Tell us the widest equipment. A gate that is two feet short is not a small mistake on a working place.
