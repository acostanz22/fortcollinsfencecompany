Vinyl Fence Installation in Fort Collins, CO

Vinyl is the low-upkeep choice for a privacy or picket line that should not need stain every few seasons. High-altitude sun and freeze-thaw are hard on thin panels, so the quote is about wall thickness, post size, and gates, not a color chip.

When vinyl is the right fence

Vinyl fits when you want a finished screen and you do not want a staining schedule.

Backyard privacy

Solid and semi-privacy vinyl blocks the neighbor’s view on a typical city lot without the gray weathering of unfinished cedar.

HOA color rules

Many newer communities in Fort Collins, Timnath, and Windsor specify a color. Vinyl holds that color closer than a stain that fades in a single summer.

Wet ground and sprinklers

The bottom of a wood fence rots where irrigation hits it. Vinyl does not, which matters on lots with spray heads along the line.

Picket fronts

A vinyl picket can satisfy a 4-foot front-yard limit without scraping and painting every other year.

How the work goes

  1. 1. Confirm style and color Privacy, semi-privacy, or picket, and the color the HOA or the house actually allows. We do not order panels before that is settled.
  2. 2. Set steel-reinforced posts where gates hang A vinyl gate on a light post will sag. Gate posts are sized to the leaf, not to the panel.
  3. 3. Build the run Rails and panels go in after posts are set and checked for line. Routes through gates and alleys are left clear.
  4. 4. Check expansion and latches Vinyl moves in heat. Gaps, gate clearances, and latches are checked so a July afternoon does not leave the gate stuck.

What changes the price

We do not publish a per-foot rate. These are the items that move an estimate for this fence.

Privacy versus picket

A solid 6-foot privacy panel uses more material than a 4-foot picket on the same footage.

Gates and post inserts

The hardware inside a vinyl gate post is a real cost. Wide gates need more of it.

Color and texture

A standard white or tan panel and a woodgrain or two-tone product are different orders.

Grade

Stepped or racked sections on a slope take more posts and more cutting than a level backyard.

Tear-out

Hauling off an old wood fence, including concrete collars, is part of the price when the new line uses the same path.

Questions about vinyl fencing

How much does vinyl fencing cost compared with wood?

Vinyl usually costs more on day one than untreated or lightly stained pine, and less over time if you would have paid to stain. The gap depends on height, gates, and whether the wood quote includes cedar or treated pine. We price both from the same measurement so you can compare the actual line, not a brochure range.

Can vinyl handle Front Range sun and winter?

Quality vinyl is made for UV and cold, which is why it is common here. Thin panels can still chalk or crack, and dark colors show heat more than white or tan. We look at the panel and the post, not a brand slogan, and we leave room for summer expansion so rails do not bind.

Do you offer privacy and semi-privacy vinyl?

Yes. Full privacy is a solid screen for backyards. Semi-privacy keeps some light and airflow, which helps on a line that catches wind. Picket profiles are available for front yards where a 6-foot wall is not allowed. The style should match the job, not a single catalog photo.

Do I need heavier posts for a vinyl gate?

Yes. Most sagging vinyl gates are a post problem, not a latch problem. A walk gate into a side yard and a double drive gate do not share hardware. If you already have a gate that droops, we can often reset that opening without replacing the whole fence.

Can you replace one cracked vinyl panel?

If the posts and rails are intact, a cracked panel is a parts repair. If the post has split or the rail has pulled out of the pocket, that bay has to be rebuilt. A photo of the crack and the nearest post is enough for us to tell you which one it is.
