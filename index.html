How a quote is built

The price comes from the line on the ground. These are the details that change it.

  1. Name the job. Screen a yard, contain an animal, secure a lot, or mark a line. That choice picks the material faster than a style photo.
  2. Check the rules for that parcel. Fort Collins, Loveland, Windsor, Timnath, and unincorporated Larimer County do not share one fence sheet. HOA rules can be stricter than the town.
  3. Count gates and grade. Slope, corners, and every opening change posts and hardware. A fence with the wrong gate is a daily problem.
  4. Decide repair or replacement. Sound posts can take new boards or mesh. Posts that have rotted or heaved cannot. Start with fence repair if the line is still mostly straight.

Questions before you call

How much does a fence cost in Fort Collins?

We do not publish a per-foot price. Footage, height, gates, slope, soil, and whether an old fence has to come out all change the number. A flat backyard is a different job from a sloped lot with an alley and two gates. Send the form or call (970) 235-8596 and we will price the line from those details.

Can you match a fence the neighborhood already uses?

Often, yes, if that material is still made and the posts are worth keeping. Newer areas around Fort Collins, Timnath, and Windsor also have HOA rules for height, color, and style. Tell us the community or send a photo of the existing line and we will say whether a match is realistic.

Do you offer wood, vinyl, aluminum, and chain link?

Yes. We install and repair wood, vinyl, aluminum, ornamental metal, chain link, picket, privacy, and farm fence, plus gates, for houses and for commercial or agricultural lots. A horse pasture and a backyard screen are not the same fence, so the material follows the job.

Do I need a permit for a fence in Fort Collins?

Inside Fort Collins, a building permit is not required for a fence 6 feet or shorter, and it is required above that. Front yards are generally limited to 4 feet and rear yards to 6 feet, with tighter rules at corners, alleys, and sidewalks. A fence next to a public right-of-way can still need a city fence permit, and an HOA can be stricter than the city. Other towns use their own rules. The city publishes the basics in its rules for common projects.

Can you repair a fence instead of replacing it?

Yes, when the posts are still sound. Missing boards, loose chain link, and a gate that will not latch are often a repair. If rot or frost has moved the posts, replacing the bad section is the honest quote. We will say which one it is after we see the fence.
