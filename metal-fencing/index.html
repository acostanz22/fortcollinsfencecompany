Metal Fence Installation in Fort Collins, CO

Metal fencing here means steel and ornamental iron, not aluminum and not chain link. It is the heavier open fence: a rigid frame, a traditional or modern picket, and a finish that has to be respected because moisture sits at the bottom of a Front Range fence all spring.

When steel or iron is the right fence

Use metal when aluminum looks too light for the house or the span, and a privacy wall is the wrong product.

Heavier ornamental lines

A steel frame holds a longer bay and a more substantial picket than a light aluminum section.

Traditional houses

Iron-style pickets fit older masonry and brick in a way a vinyl panel does not.

Slopes that need a rigid line

Steel can be racked or custom-fit. It does not flap the way a loose chain-link fabric does in wind.

Not a zero-maintenance promise

Coatings fail at cuts and at the ground line. Metal is strong. It is not the same upkeep story as aluminum.

How the work goes

  1. 1. Separate it from aluminum We confirm you want steel or iron, not an aluminum look-alike. The price and the upkeep are different.
  2. 2. Layout and grade Posts, corners, and gates are marked. Slope decides whether sections rack or are built to the grade.
  3. 3. Set and weld or assemble Frames are installed square. Field cuts are coated so a raw edge is not left in the weather.
  4. 4. Hardware check Latches and hinges are set so the heavier leaf does not sag the post in the first season.

What changes the price

We do not publish a per-foot rate. These are the items that move an estimate for this fence.

Steel versus aluminum

Steel costs more to build and more to finish. If the only goal is an open black picket, aluminum is often the better buy.

Finish

Powder coat, paint, and galvanizing are different products. A raw cut in the field has to be sealed.

Pickets and height

Closer picket spacing and a taller panel add weight, posts, and labor.

Gates

A steel drive gate is heavy. The post and the hinge are priced for that weight.

Access

Long steel sections are harder to carry through a side yard than aluminum. Tight access can add time.

Questions about metal fencing

How much does ornamental steel cost versus aluminum?

Steel is the more expensive open fence on most Fort Collins lots because the material, the posts, and the finish all cost more. Aluminum wins when you want the iron look without the coating maintenance. We quote them as two options on the same layout if you are actually deciding between them.

Can you install steel on a slope?

Yes. Sections can be racked or fabricated to the grade so the bottom bar follows the ground. A steep lot takes more posts and more fitting than a flat one. That is a layout issue we price after we see the hill, not a reason to avoid metal.

Do you offer a wrought-iron look?

Yes. Ornamental steel and iron are the traditional version of that look. Aluminum is the lighter version. If the house is older brick or stone and you want the heavier frame, steel is the material. If you want to avoid repainting, say so before we order it.

Do I need to paint a metal fence in this climate?

A factory finish should not need paint on day one. Snow, sprinklers, and dog urine at the bottom rail are where coatings fail later. Touch-up at chips and at the ground line is normal ownership of steel. If you do not want that task, aluminum is the clearer choice.

Can you repair a rusted or bent section?

A bent picket or a broken weld on one bay is a repair. Rust that has scaled the bottom of every post is a replacement conversation. We treat the bad bay and leave the sound fence in place when the metal is still solid.
