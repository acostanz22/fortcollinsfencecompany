Wood Fence Installation in Fort Collins, CO

Wood is still the usual backyard fence in Fort Collins: cedar or treated pine, built as privacy, shadowbox, or rail. It can be stained to the house. It also needs posts set below the frost line and a plan for sun, because unfinished wood grays fast at this elevation.

When a wood fence is the right job

Wood is the right call when you want a screen you can stain, repair board by board, and match to an older neighborhood.

Privacy on a city lot

A 6-foot board fence is the standard rear-yard screen where the city allows that height. Front yards are a different, shorter fence.

Shadowbox for wind

Alternating boards let some air through. On an exposed lot that matters, because a solid wall takes the full gust.

Older neighborhoods

Many mid-town and northwest lots already have cedar. Matching the existing spacing and cap rail looks intentional. A vinyl panel next to weathered cedar rarely does.

Dogs and kids

A solid wood line with a flush bottom and a latch that closes is a practical yard fence. The gate width still has to clear the mower.

How the work goes

  1. 1. Choose the build Privacy, shadowbox, or rail, plus the gate locations. We confirm height against the yard: front, side, rear, or corner.
  2. 2. Set posts in concrete Posts go deep enough for frost heave and for the wind load of the style you picked. Gate posts are larger.
  3. 3. Hang rails and boards Rails are fastened to stay straight. Boards are spaced for the style, with a kickboard where irrigation would soak the bottoms.
  4. 4. Leave it ready to finish New wood can be left to dry before stain, or stained if the moisture is right. We will say which, instead of painting wet lumber.

What changes the price

We do not publish a per-foot rate. These are the items that move an estimate for this fence.

Cedar versus treated pine

Cedar costs more up front and handles weather with less chemical treatment. Pine is the lower-cost privacy board and still needs stain or it will twist.

Style

Solid privacy uses more board than shadowbox or a two-rail line of the same length.

Gates

A framed wood gate with a wheel or a heavier hinge set is separate from the panel price.

Grade and corners

Stepping down a slope adds posts. Each corner is a structural post, not a place to skip.

Stain and tear-out

Staining can be part of the job or left to you. Removing an old fence with concrete footings is extra.

Questions about wood fencing

How much does a wood privacy fence cost in Fort Collins?

Footage, wood species, gates, and the ground decide it. A level backyard in treated pine is the simple version. Cedar, a slope, two gates, and hauling off the old fence are the version that changes the number. We measure before we quote, and we do not give a per-foot price that ignores those items.

Can you use cedar instead of treated pine?

Yes. Cedar is the better board if you want a stain color and a fence that is less likely to twist in dry summer air. Treated pine is a sound privacy fence at a lower material cost, and it still needs a finish if you care how it looks in two years. We will price the same layout in both.

Do you offer shadowbox, board-on-board, and picket?

Yes. Solid privacy blocks the view. Shadowbox and board-on-board give privacy with a finished face on both sides and a bit of airflow. Picket is the shorter front-yard version. They are different builds, not different names for one panel.

Do I need to stain a new wood fence right away?

Not always. Lumber that is still wet should dry before stain, or the finish peels. Fort Collins sun will gray an unfinished fence quickly once it is dry, so the wait is weeks, not years. We will tell you if the boards on your job are ready.

Can you replace rotten posts and reuse the boards?

Sometimes. If the boards are straight and the rot is only at a few posts, resetting those posts is the repair. If the boards are cupped, split, or nailed to a line that has heaved out of plumb, new boards are the honest fix. We will not sell a full replacement when the wood is still good.
